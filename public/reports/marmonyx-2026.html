Critical
Applications Section — 8 Dead Menu Items
The entire Applications navigation section uses javascript:void(0) as the link destination. Benchtops, Splashbacks, Flooring, Walls, and Staircases — the highest-intent pages on the site — do not exist. These represent the core buyer journeys and are all invisible to Google.
High
Brand Name Misspellings in Navigation
"Caesare stone" should be Caesarstone. "Sile stone" should be Silestone. These are registered brand names. Misspelling them misses branded search traffic and signals poor attention to detail to clients who know the products.
High
All Social Icons Link to # on Every Page
Every social media icon — Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, WhatsApp — in both the header and footer links to #. No actual profiles are connected to the website despite those profiles existing and containing genuine project content.
Medium
Copyright Footer Reads 2022
The footer displays © 2022, three years out of date. A small detail but one that signals an unmaintained website to both visitors and search engines. The Google+ share button on the About page compounds this — Google+ closed in 2019.
Medium
Joomla Plugin URL Architecture
Product pages use paths like /hikashop-menu-for-categories-listing/product/38-... and /component/phocagallery/.... These plugin-generated URLs carry no topical authority and limit SEO improvement without a migration.
Pass
GTM Installed · Canonical Tags Set Correctly
Google Tag Manager (GTM-WPDPCB9) is present on every page. Canonical tags consistently point to the www subdomain. The phone number in the top bar is a working tel: link. Slider images have descriptive filenames and alt text — the strongest technical SEO element on the site.

Stage Three

Brand Voice, Copy Quality & Content Gaps

Marmonyx has a strong engineering-led brand story. It appears on exactly one page. The remaining site copy was written in a different era for a different brand and was never updated. The result is three conflicting voices across five pages.

Voice Inconsistency — Same Website, Two Different Businesses
Homepage — Old Copy (Still Live)
"Well, aside from the products we offer, our commitment to customer service that makes purchases a snap, We also happen to adore stone... you can walk on it, run your hand across it, you can feel the cool texture and really get a sense of where it came from."
Problem: Hobbyist language. No technical proof. No location. No differentiator. Positions the business as a stone enthusiast, not a precision fabrication specialist.
"The colours of stone cannot be mixed; they are completely unique and back to days of ancient antiquity."
Problem: "Back to days of ancient antiquity" is redundant and grammatically incomplete. Three sentences that convey zero commercial intent.
About Page — Updated Copy
"Led by a Mechanical Engineer with over 18 years of specialised expertise, we approach every slab with a level of rigour that traditional masonry cannot match."
This works: Technical credential front-loaded. Clear differentiator. Confident without being boastful. This is the voice the entire site should use.
"We are material-agnostic master fabricators. By focusing on the specialised processes of fabrication and installation, we provide a limitless range of options."
This works: "Material-agnostic" is a genuine differentiator and a phrase no competitor is using. Clear value proposition.
Critical
Footer Copy — Errors on Every Page
Footer copy — appears on every page of the site

"We specialize in the precision fabrication of Marble, Granite, Quartzite, and Sintered Stone, Porcelain — creating luxury kitchen benchtops, bespoke vanities, and complex architectural features. Where others see a challenge, we provide innovative engineering solutions for Sydney's finest homes!"

Three errors: (1) American English — "specialize" should be "specialise". (2) The comma structure "Sintered Stone, Porcelain" is ambiguous — needs "and". (3) Ends with an exclamation mark, which undercuts the authority of everything before it. The final line is actually the strongest copy on the site — it belongs on the homepage hero, not buried in a footer.
High
Gallery Page — Zero Written Content
The Projects page is a thumbnail grid with no surrounding copy. No project names. No suburb. No stone type. No scope. The gallery contains evidence of extraordinary work — backlit onyx staircases, curved Calacatta Viola islands, commercial fitouts, overseas quarry sourcing — none of which is described or contextualised in any way. Every image is a case study that has never been written.
High
Zero Social Proof on the Website
There are no client testimonials, reviews, or quotes anywhere on the site. No star rating. No Houzz badge displayed. No Google review embed. For a high-consideration purchase like a bespoke stone benchtop, reviews are the primary trust signal. Every competitor in this category prominently features them.
Medium
No Service Area Copy — Key Suburbs Invisible
The meta description mentions "Eastern Suburbs, North Shore & Inner West" but these suburbs appear nowhere in the actual page copy. Sydney's high-value renovation postcodes — Paddington, Mosman, Woollahra, Neutral Bay, Balmain — are entirely absent from the site text. These are the suburbs where this business's ideal clients live.
Medium
Trade Licence Missing from Website
The stonemason contractor licence number (342859C) is listed on the Houzz profile but does not appear anywhere on the website. For a licensed contractor doing high-value residential work, the licence number is both a trust signal and a consumer protection requirement that many clients look for.
Medium
No Process Page — How We Work
There is no page or section explaining the client journey. The engineering narrative is on the About page but the practical sequence — enquire, consult, measure, select stone, fabricate, install — is never laid out. This creates uncertainty at the decision point. A client who does not understand the process is less likely to commit to an enquiry.

The site has one primary lead capture mechanism — a form with six fields and no surrounding context. Multiple contact points are broken. Tracking is not configured. There is no secondary engagement path for visitors who are not yet ready to enquire.

There is also a second CTA — "Contact Us" in the nav — pointing to a different page with a different form. Two competing primary actions. No secondary engagement path for visitors who are not yet ready to enquire.
Critical
WhatsApp Link — Broken on Every Page
The WhatsApp icon appears in the footer social block sitewide. It links to #. For a luxury stone business targeting inner-Sydney homeowners, WhatsApp is the primary way this client type sends inspiration images, photos of their space, and quick questions. A working wa.me/ link with a pre-filled message costs five minutes to fix.
Critical
Footer Phone Number Does Not Dial on Mobile
The phone number in the top bar is correctly formatted as a tel: link and dials on mobile. The same number in the footer links to #. On a mobile device, tapping the footer number does nothing. This is a broken conversion point repeated on every single page of the site.
Critical
Quote Form — No Context, No Trust, No Guidance
The get-a-quote page contains six form fields and nothing else. No headline. No reassurance. No response time commitment. No testimonial near the submission point. No explanation of the process. No guidance on the attachment field. The "Products enquiring about" field is a freetext box — no dropdown or checkbox for project type. A visitor landing here from a high-intent search has no reason to trust the outcome of submitting.
Critical
No Conversion Tracking — GTM Is Dormant
GTM is installed but no conversion tags appear to be configured inside it. Form submissions are not being tracked as conversion events. There is no Google Ads tag, no Meta Pixel, no GA4 event tracking for enquiries. Without this data, there is no way to know which pages or traffic sources are generating leads — and any future advertising spend will be flying completely blind.
High
CTA Inconsistency — Two Actions, Two Destinations
The homepage hero uses "Ask Us" (→ /get-a-quote.html). The homepage body and navigation use "Contact Us" (→ /contact.html). Both destinations have different forms. A visitor who clicks both links — as many do when evaluating a site — encounters two different experiences with no explanation of the difference. There is no defined primary conversion goal.
High
No Showroom Booking Option
The Marrickville workshop is open Monday to Saturday. The hours are published in the footer. There is no way to book a visit online — no calendar, no Calendly link, no "visit our showroom" CTA anywhere on the site. For a bespoke fabrication business, the showroom visit is a critical step in the sales process. The site does not present it as an option.

The Core Social Finding

The work is already there. The audience is not.

A curved marble island with a bullnose edge in Paddington. A backlit onyx staircase that most Sydney stonemasons cannot fabricate. An overseas quarry sourcing trip that no competitor is showing. These are not ordinary content — they are category-defining proof points sitting in front of fewer than 500 people. The social media challenge here is not content creation. It is distribution, consistency, and a systematic approach to growing the audience of interior designers, architects, builders, and premium homeowners who would act on seeing this work.
