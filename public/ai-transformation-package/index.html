Methodology

Assess, align, adopt

These are monday.com's three phases. We stay in the work with you until people are using the workflow on a normal day, with a weekly check-in, and nothing goes live until you've signed it off.

01

Phase one, about a quarter of the time

Assess

We learn the business before we touch a board, across one to three sessions, and a short conversation with one or two leaders sets the outcomes in their own words.

  • Readiness scored 1 to 5 before we start
  • What the team owns, this year's priorities, and what great looks like
  • The current process, or the target if it doesn't exist yet
  • Each pain sorted: classify and route, extract and enrich, assist and generate, or automate and act
  • An agent first, and a written reason when an agent is the wrong tool
  • A SMART objective: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound
02

Phase two, about half the time

Align

We design the target workflow and prove it on a small slice of real items before we build the rest.

  • Solution design tied to the objective, not to a feature list
  • A proof on items from your account
  • Edge cases tested, including what the agent must not do
  • Credit use checked against the budget you agreed
  • Written sign-off before the full build
  • A weekly check-in until go-live
03

Phase three, about a quarter of the time

Adopt

People need to know why it exists, want to use it, know how, and still be using it on a normal day after we've stepped back.

  • Training for the people who use it, and a deeper session for the champion
  • Instructions for the workflow, the agent, and the prompts
  • Go-live watch, with issues fixed while we are still on the engagement
  • The baseline read back against the target
  • Handover, and a date for the next conversation

The pieces

What each tool actually does

We don't switch all of these on. We pick the ones the process needs, and we tell you the credit cost before they run.

Agents

For work that needs a judgment across steps, like routing a request, chasing a missing field, or updating the next board. We try an agent first, with a basic one as a short chain and an advanced one reasoning across boards.

Basic, 10 to 50 credits. Advanced, 50 to 250 or more

Sidekick

The assistant inside the account, for when someone needs an answer from the boards right now rather than a standing automation, and it spends credits from the first message.

30 to 150 or more credits a message

AI blocks

One step on one item, such as summarising, pulling a field out of an email, tagging a request, or reading the tone, which is enough when a full agent is more than the job.

8 credits an item

Vibe

A small app on monday.com for when the board is the wrong screen, built for that process and either public or private, with the app itself about US$10 and the prompts spending the credits.

About 30 to 500 credits a prompt

AI workflows

The chain that connects agents, blocks, and the rules you already have, so a job can move from one team to the next without a handover meeting.

Credits per run, by complexity

AI notetaker

A meeting becomes notes and actions on the item, which is the right pick when the lost work is what was agreed in the room rather than the board itself.

120 credits an hour

What good looks like

By the numbers

We agree one to three outcomes with one or two leaders, in their words, and we don't promise a return. We write the baseline first, then read it back on a live dashboard at the end.

Productivity

Time back on the repeated job

We start here because it is the clearest thing to measure, and it doesn't need a finance model.

Example target: status reporting down by a set number of hours each week

Quality

Fewer errors, the same output

We count missed fields, rework, and jobs that come out of the process in a different shape every time.

Example target: exception rate on that workflow down against the baseline

Adoption

People using it on a normal day

We look at who is active, and how often the agent runs, because a workflow nobody opens is not finished.

Example target: the champion's team on it through the watch period

Business impact

The result you named

Cycle time or response time, only if you raised it, and we don't introduce headcount as the goal.

Example target: the number from the alignment conversation
