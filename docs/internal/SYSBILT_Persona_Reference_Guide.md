## 1. Why We Have Personas

A persona is not a profile. It's a targeting decision. Before you write anything you need to know who you're writing for and what state of mind they're in when they read it.

SYSBILT has four personas. Three of them map to the three phases a business moves through as it grows. The fourth sees the whole system at once. They're defined by state of mind, not job title.

The four:

- **The Builder** (Get Clients). The business works but it's invisible online and leads are slipping away.
- **The Scaler** (Scale Faster). Revenue is real but the founder is the bottleneck and growth means more hours.
- **The Controller** (See Clearly). The money's there but they're flying blind, with data scattered across platforms.
- **The Visionary** (the whole system). They know the operation is held together with tape and they want one partner to modernise it over time.

How to pick:

- Writing for a broad or cold audience, so social, homepage, blog, or email, use one of the three phase personas. Pick one and commit.
- Writing a service or system page, use the phase persona that owns that service.
- Writing a proposal or pitching the full connected build, use the Visionary.
- Need a concrete real-world example to make a point land, pull the "Who it's for" line from the Services and Pricing Master. Don't invent a new persona.

Persona names never appear in the copy itself. The reader should feel spoken to, not labelled.

## 2. The Builder

Get Clients phase

*"I know I'm losing work. I just don't know where it goes."*

**Who they are**

- Owners running a real operation, a clinic, a practice, a studio, a high-end build firm, with a weak or nonexistent digital presence.
- Revenue is often real but dependent on word of mouth or referrals. The tap could turn off tomorrow.
- Leads come in by phone, text, or email with nothing capturing or organising them.

**Their pain**

- The silent competitor. They miss a call because they're mid-appointment, and the enquiry goes to the next result on Google in 30 seconds.
- The after-hours leak. Customers search at 8pm. The owner is asleep. The lead vanishes.
- The black hole pipeline. They send a proposal and forget to follow up. The deal dies in the inbox.

**What they need**

A digital front door that captures leads, a CRM that organises them, and automation that follows up without them having to remember. The Get Clients services: Websites and E-commerce, CRM and Lead Tracking, Automation.

**Where they are**

Instagram, referral networks, industry associations, and local business groups. They're not LinkedIn power users. Catch them in the gaps of a day spent running the operation.

**Awareness and how to open**

They know they have a problem but may not know a system is the fix. Lead with benefit. Show them the world they want, then drop into the pain in the first body line.

**Sales hook example**

*"You're losing work because you're mid-consult when the phone rings. We'll install a system that texts the caller back the second you can't answer. You win the booking without leaving the room."*

## 3. The Scaler

Scale Faster phase

*"The only way I can do more is if there are two of me."*

**Who they are**

- Operators who've proven the model. Revenue is consistent. But the business can't grow without the founder in every decision.
- They've likely bought software already. Some of it sits unused because the team found it too hard or nobody trained them properly.
- They feel the ceiling every week. They know the answer isn't hiring more people. They want leverage.

**Their pain**

- The Groundhog Day interruption. They answer the same questions from staff 50 times a week and can't get an hour of unbroken focus.
- The creator-mode conflict. Setting up a camera, editing video, and writing captions ruins their day. The friction to post is too high.
- The shelfware graveyard. They're paying for tools nobody uses because adoption was never built.

**What they need**

AI and automation to handle the repetitive work, a content system that doesn't eat their weekend, and training so the team actually uses the tools. The Scale Faster services: AI Assistants, Content Systems, Team Training.

**Where they are**

LinkedIn, business podcasts, and founder peer groups. They're actively looking for leverage and will engage with content that names their exact situation.

**Awareness and how to open**

Fully aware of the problem. Lead with pain. Name the frustration directly and they'll lean in.

**Sales hook example**

*"You're paying brilliant people to do robotic work. Every time your team copies data from an email into a spreadsheet you're leaking profit and burning out your best people. We'll automate the admin and make sure the team actually uses what we build. You double your capacity without hiring another data-entry clerk."*

## 4. The Controller

See Clearly phase

*"I run a million-dollar business and I'm still guessing half the time."*

**Who they are**

- Established operators with real revenue who've outgrown intuition as a management tool.
- They check multiple platforms every morning and none of the numbers match. Login fatigue is real.
- Often weighing an exit or a raise. They need the business to look as good as it actually performs.

**Their pain**

- The attribution trap. They're spending on ads and can't prove which channel drives net profit. They're scared of scaling the wrong thing.
- The rearview mirror. They only find out about a sales drop or a hidden problem after the damage is done.
- The valuation discount. Buyers pay a premium for clean, predictable numbers. Messy data lowers the price.

**What they need**

A unified dashboard that pulls everything into one place and shows what's working, what isn't, and where to focus. Dashboards and Reporting at the centre, connected back to every other service.

**Where they are**

LinkedIn, CFO and founder communities, and exit-planning conversations. They engage with data-led content and clear before-and-after case studies.

**Awareness and how to open**

Acutely aware. They feel this every time they open a spreadsheet. Lead with pain, and with the contrast between what they have now and what certainty looks like.

**Sales hook example**

*"You're steering a big ship through a foggy window. Thousands of rows of data and zero actual intelligence. Right now you make expensive calls based on what happened last month. We'll build your control tower. One screen, everything connected. You stop reacting to the past and start predicting the future."*

## 5. The Visionary

The whole system, cross-phase

*"I know the whole thing is held together with tape. I want one team to fix it, not five."*

**Who they are**

- An owner or managing director of an established business who has decided the whole operation needs sorting, not one piece.
- They've found product-market fit and built a team, but they've hit a ceiling. The tech is a patchwork: a website, a CRM, a spreadsheet, an accounting tool, a dozen apps, none of them talking to each other.
- They don't want five agencies. They want one partner who can audit the mess and take ownership of the fix.

**How they buy**

This is the connection-sight buyer, the reason the differentiator exists. They usually start by testing us with one piece, a website, a CRM, a single automation. If they like the work and the way we think, they hand over more, and the relationship grows into the full modernisation over time. It doesn't have to be the whole engine on day one. The point is they can see the whole picture, and they want a partner who sees it too.

**Their pain**

- The Frankenstein stack. Thousands a month on software, and staff still doing manual data entry because nothing connects.
- The complexity ceiling. The business has grown too complex to hold in their head. Balls get dropped, and there's a constant low hum of anxiety that something is breaking out of sight.

**What they want**

Executive calm. Wake up, look at one dashboard, know exactly what the business is doing, and trust the machinery underneath is running without their hands on it. They're buying a partner as much as the technology, someone to act as a fractional operations and technology lead.

**What they need**

The connected system, built in stages on a roadmap. This maps to the Digital Transformation engagement, but the way in is usually a single piece that proves the value first.

**Where they are**

LinkedIn, founder peer groups, and referrals from people we've already helped. They respond to the whole-system story and to proof that the pieces connect.

**Awareness and how to open**

They know they have a problem and they've usually named it themselves. Don't oversell the mechanics. Sell the calm, the single point of accountability, and the fact they can start small and expand.

**Sales hook example**

*"You've built a great business, but right now it runs on tape and your own willpower. You don't need another subscription. You need the pieces to finally talk to each other. Start with one. If it works the way we say it will, we connect the rest, one stage at a time, until the whole thing runs without you in every decision."*

## 7. The Website Funnel Model

The site is a three-stage awareness funnel. Each page type has a different job. The persona, the copy approach, and the call to action all depend on where the reader sits.

**Stage 1: Homepage (Awareness)**

Speaks to cold or warm visitors deciding whether SYSBILT is relevant. Touch all three phase personas in the hero, one tight line of pain each, one for the Builder, one for the Scaler, one for the Controller. Benefit-first headline, pain in the first body line, then offer the path. CTA is soft: explore the system, find your phase, book a Systems Audit to talk it through. The rest of the page lets each persona follow their own thread.

**Stage 2: System pages (Consideration)**

One page per phase: Get Clients, Scale Faster, See Clearly. Visitors land here once they've self-selected. Write to the one phase persona for that system. Open with the benefit of the phase, confirm the pain, describe the system in plain language, and make clear they can start with one piece and build. CTA is directional but flexible: see what's inside this system, talk to us about where to start. The most important message is that it isn't all-or-nothing. The system grows with them.

**Stage 3: Service pages (Conversion)**

Speak to visitors actively looking for a specific solution. Use the phase persona that owns the service: the Get Clients services speak to the Builder, the Scale Faster services to the Scaler, dashboards to the Controller. Open with their exact frustration, describe their world so they feel understood, then present the solution with proof. CTA is hard and specific: book a free Systems Audit, start here. Be direct, name the solution, show what it produces, get out of the way.

**The transformation pitch**

Anything selling the full connected build, a proposal, a high-value pitch, the Digital Transformation page, speaks to the Visionary. Whole-system story, staged roadmap, one partner accountable.

Funnel summary:

| Page type | Stage | Persona | Copy approach | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Homepage | Awareness | All three phase personas | Benefit headline, pain in body | Soft, exploratory |
| System pages | Consideration | One phase persona per system | Benefit then pain, system overview | Directional, flexible |
| Service pages | Conversion | The phase persona that owns the service | Pain-first, full depth, proof | Hard and specific |
| Transformation, proposals | Conversion | The Visionary | Whole-system story, staged roadmap | One partner, start small |

## 8. Awareness Levels: How to Open

Once you know the persona, you need to know how aware they are of the problem. That sets how you open.

**Not aware yet**

They don't know they have a problem, or they haven't named it. Open with benefit. Show them the world they want and let the desire come first. Example: *"A business that runs without you in every decision."* Use for cold audiences who don't know SYSBILT yet, and top-of-funnel brand content.

**Aware of the problem**

They feel it every day. Name it directly and they'll lean in, because nobody has described their situation that clearly. Example: *"You built the business. Now the business runs you."* Use for website copy, service pages, retargeting, and email.

**Looking for a solution**

They already know what they need. Don't oversell. Be direct, show what we build and what the result looks like. Example: *"Here's what the system looks like. Here's what it produces. Book a call."* Use for service pages and anyone in active buying mode.

**The strongest structure**

Benefit in the headline, pain in the first line of body copy. This is the default for the homepage, service pages, and any long-form where you don't know exactly where the reader sits. Example: headline *"A business that runs without you,"* first line *"Right now it doesn't, and you feel that every single day."*

## 9. Quick Reference: Which Persona for What

- **LinkedIn post.** A phase persona. The Scaler and the Controller are most active here. Pick one and commit.
- **Instagram post or reel.** A phase persona. The Builder and the Scaler. Visual pain points, before and after, systems in action.
- **Homepage.** Touch all three phase personas in the hero, one line each. Let the rest of the page serve whoever self-selects.
- **System page.** The phase persona for that system only. Stress that they can start with one piece and expand.
- **Service page.** The phase persona that owns the service. Open with their pain, describe their world, present the service as the way out. Pull a concrete example from the Services Master if you need one.
- **Blog post.** Pick one phase persona and write straight to them. Don't try to cover all of them in one post.
- **Email sequence.** Work out which phase the recipient is in and use that persona. Name their situation in the subject line.
- **Case study.** Identify the phase persona that fits the client, and use the Services Master "Who it's for" line to set the scene. Structure: the problem, what we built, the result.
- **Proposal or full-system pitch.** The Visionary. The whole connected build, the staged roadmap, start small and grow.

---

## 10. How Not to Use Personas

- Don't speak to more than one phase persona in a single post or email. Pick one and commit.
- Don't invent new personas. There are four. If you need an industry-specific example, pull it from the Services Master, don't promote it to a persona.
- Don't use persona names in the copy itself. The reader should feel spoken to, not labelled.
- Don't pick a persona after you've written the content and then try to match it. Pick first, then write.
- Don't drift back to the old seven pillar archetypes. They're deprecated. If you find them still live anywhere, flag it.
- Don't frame any persona as a tradie or blue-collar operator, and don't use trade imagery in hooks or examples. The Builder is a premium operator with a weak digital presence, not a trade stereotype.

*"Know who you're writing for before you write the first word. Everything else follows from that."*
